02 · What gets examined

Five things, in one week.

Support problems rarely sit in one place. A slow reply time can be a staffing problem, a tooling problem or a billing page that confuses people. We look at all five so the answer is the real one.

People
  • Staffing levels against real demand
  • Schedules and cover
  • Quality checks
  • Skills and training gaps
Processes
  • Escalation paths
  • Day to day workflows
  • Reply time targets
  • What makes people contact you twice
Technology
  • Helpdesk setup
  • Automation and what it answers
  • WhatsApp and other channels
  • CRM and integrations
Customers
  • What drives contacts
  • How hard you are to deal with
  • Complaints and where they go
Economics
  • Cost per contact
  • Cost per customer
  • Cost per channel
  • Where the money actually goes

03 · What you get

A report you can act on without us.

The written report

Plain English, no jargon, no slides. What we found, what it is costing you, and the evidence for both. Usually fifteen to twenty pages.

The ranked problem list

Every problem in order of what it costs you, not in order of how easy it is to fix. You can see immediately what is worth attention.

Costed fixes

What each fix involves, roughly what it costs, and what it should save. Some are free and take an afternoon. Some take months.

The report is yours. It is written so your own team, or another consultant, could pick it up and do the work. Talk to us about an audit →

04 · Who it suits

It breaks differently at every size.

The audit is the same week of work at any volume. What changes is where the problems turn out to be.

A few hundred messages a month

The audit is short and mostly about what is missing. Usually there is no record of what came in, so the first job is counting it.

A few thousand messages a month

This is where the audit earns its money. You have hired people, quality varies by who picks up, and nobody can say what customers are contacting about.

Tens of thousands messages a month

The focus moves to contact drivers and staffing models. Headcount has been growing with volume, and the same problems generate messages every month.

Hundreds of thousands messages a month

Structural work. Outsourced partners, self service that does not work, and automation bought before anyone understood what to automate.

06 · Questions

What people ask about the audit.

How long does it take?

One week from the day we get access. Some of that is reading your data, some is listening to how your team actually works. You get the report at the end of it.

What do you need from us?

Read only access to your inbox and helpdesk, whatever reporting you already have, and about two hours of your team's time spread across the week. We work around them, not the other way round.

What if we have no numbers at all?

Most businesses do not. Establishing them is the first part of the work and it is included. If your systems cannot report something, we say so and tell you what it would take to fix that.

Do we have to buy the fixes from you?

No. The report is yours to keep and it is written so another person could pick it up and do the work. Plenty of clients hand it to their own team.

What does it cost?

A fixed price agreed before anything starts, based on the size of your operation. You get a straight number on the free call. Never hourly, never a surprise.

Is this different from the free call?

Yes. The free call is thirty minutes and gives you an honest read on your three biggest problems. The audit is a week of work with a written report, ranked problems and costed fixes behind it.

Worth thirty minutes to find out?

Start with the free call. You'll leave it knowing your three biggest problems and roughly what they're costing, whether we end up working together or not.
