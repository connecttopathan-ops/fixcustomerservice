The cheapest way to reduce customer support costs is to get fewer contacts, not to answer them more cheaply. Most businesses start at the wrong end. They cut staff, buy a chatbot or outsource, and service gets worse while costs creep back up.

Do it in this order instead. Remove the reasons customers contact you. Fix the problems that make them contact you twice. Then automate what is left. The team gets smaller as a result of less work, not as a way to create it.

## Why the order matters

Your support cost is roughly the number of contacts multiplied by the cost of handling each one. Most cost-cutting advice attacks the second number. It makes each contact cheaper with software, cheaper staff or a bot.

The first number is usually the bigger opportunity. In most businesses we look at, a large share of contacts should never have happened. Think of "where is my order?" or "did my payment go through?" Each one is a small failure somewhere else in the business that support pays for.

If you automate or outsource before fixing those, you lock in paying for them forever.

## The four steps

### 1. Remove the reasons customers get in touch

Tag every contact with a reason for two weeks. Then take your top five reasons and ask one question about each: what would stop the customer needing to ask?

- "Where is my order?" often means your dispatch email lacks a tracking link.
- "Did my payment go through?" often means the confirmation page is unclear.
- "How do I change my address?" often means the option is hidden in the account page.

These fixes usually sit with other teams, such as operations, product or finance. That is why support costs rarely fall when support is left to fix them alone. We explain the number to track in [how to calculate cost per contact](/blog/how-to-calculate-cost-per-contact).

### 2. Stop customers contacting you twice

A customer who writes again about the same problem costs you double. Usually the agent couldn't see the order, couldn't make the decision, or had to pass it to another team.

Give agents the information and the authority to finish the job in one go. Set a clear refund limit they can approve without asking. Put order and payment details next to the conversation. We covered why this matters more than speed in [reply time is not the real problem](/blog/reply-time-is-not-the-real-problem).

### 3. Match your team to when contacts arrive

Most teams are staffed for the average day. But contacts arrive in peaks, often Monday mornings and after a delivery problem. The result is idle time in the afternoon and long queues at 9am.

Count contacts by hour for a month and move shifts to match. This alone often lets the same team handle more without overtime.

### 4. Automate what is left

Now automation pays off, because you are only automating questions that genuinely need an answer. Good candidates are simple, frequent and factual: order status, opening hours, return steps.

Measure your chatbot on problems solved, not chats started. A bot that passes most chats to a person adds cost. And if you run support on WhatsApp, every bot message now has a price. See [WhatsApp pricing from 1 October 2026](/blog/whatsapp-pricing-october-2026).

That is €10,000 a month saved, or 34%, without anyone answering faster or working harder. In practice, the smaller team usually comes from not replacing people who leave and not hiring as you grow. It rarely needs redundancies.

This matches what we have seen in real operations. In one B2B payroll business we rebuilt, incoming requests fell by 45% and the team got 30% smaller. First response targets were met 98% of the time, up from 75%.

## Four mistakes that make costs worse

1. **Cutting staff first.** Queues grow, customers chase, and the extra "any update?" contacts eat the saving.
2. **Hiding your contact details.** Customers still need help. They just arrive angrier, on a channel that costs more, or in a one-star review.
3. **Outsourcing a broken process.** You pay someone else to answer the same avoidable questions, and you lose sight of why they happen.
4. **Buying a bot before tagging contacts.** You can't automate well what you haven't counted.

## Where to start this week

Pick one channel and tag the reason for every contact for the next two weeks. Nothing else in this guide works well until you know why customers get in touch.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the fastest way to reduce customer support costs?

Find your top five reasons for contact and remove the cause of one of them. Fixing one common reason, such as missing tracking links, can cut contacts within weeks.

### Will a chatbot reduce my support costs?

Only if it solves problems rather than passing them on. Automate after you have removed avoidable contacts, and measure the bot on problems solved.

### Is outsourcing customer support cheaper?

The hourly rate often is. The total cost may not be, if the process behind it creates avoidable and repeat contacts. Fix the process first, then compare.

### How much can I realistically save?

It depends on how many of your contacts are avoidable. In the example above, the saving is about a third. In operations we have rebuilt, incoming requests fell by close to half.
