The WhatsApp Business app is free and works well when one or two people answer customers from a phone. The WhatsApp Business API is built for a team: many people on one number, chatbots, reports and links to your other systems. It costs money per message and needs a provider or a developer to set up.

Most small businesses start on the app. The question is when it stops being enough.

## The two in plain words

**The WhatsApp Business app** is the green app you download from the app store. You install it on one phone with your business number. You can also link up to four other devices, such as a laptop or a tablet, so a few people can read and reply. It has a business profile, a product catalogue, labels, quick replies, and automatic greeting and away messages.

**The WhatsApp Business API** (Meta calls it the WhatsApp Business Platform) has no app of its own. You connect your number to a platform, such as a helpdesk or a shared inbox, through a provider or directly through Meta. Your team then answers WhatsApp messages inside that platform, next to email and other channels.

## Side by side

| | WhatsApp Business app | WhatsApp Business API |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | Free | Per message, plus the provider's fee |
| Who can answer | One phone plus up to 4 linked devices | As many agents as your platform allows |
| Assigning chats to people | No | Yes |
| Chatbot | Only greeting and away messages | Full chatbot, including AI |
| Reports (volumes, reply times) | No | Yes, through your platform |
| Links to your CRM or order system | No | Yes |
| Messaging customers after 24 hours | Yes, as normal chat | Only with a pre-approved template, charged per message |
| Set-up time | Minutes | Days to a few weeks |

One point in that table catches people out. On the API, once 24 hours pass since the customer's last message, you can only restart the chat with a template that Meta has approved. On the app, you just type. The API is more powerful, but it has more rules.

From 1 October 2026 the API also charges for normal support replies. We explain what that means in [WhatsApp pricing from 1 October 2026](/blog/whatsapp-pricing-october-2026).

## Stay on the app if

- One or two people answer every message.
- You get fewer than about 30 chats a day.
- You don't need to know your reply times or chat volumes.
- Nobody else in the business needs to see the conversations.

For many shops, salons and small service businesses, this describes them well. The app is a good tool at this size. Don't pay for more until you need it.

## Move to the API when

- **Chats get lost.** Two people reply to the same customer, or nobody does, because nobody knows who owns the chat.
- **You can't answer basic questions about your support.** How many chats did we get last week? How long did customers wait? The app can't tell you.
- **The same questions come in all day.** A chatbot can answer order status or opening hours, but only on the API.
- **Customers repeat themselves.** Your team can't see order details next to the chat, so they ask for the order number every time.
- **More than four people need access.** The app's device limit becomes a daily problem.

When we moved a payroll card business from email to WhatsApp on the API, the chatbot answered 69% of chats without an agent. The average wait time fell by 96%. That was only possible because the API allowed a bot, handover to the right agent with the full history, and reports on every chat. You can read the [published case study by Infobip](https://www.infobip.com/customer/edenred).

You no longer have to give up the app to do this. Meta now lets you connect your app number to the API and keep using the app on it. Providers call this [coexistence](https://docs.360dialog.com/partner/onboarding/whatsapp-coexistence). Recent chat history can be synced across. There are two catches. Linked devices are unlinked when you connect. And you have to open the app at least once every 13 days to keep it active.

One more thing to plan for: write your templates early. You will need approved templates for order updates and for restarting chats after 24 hours, and approval can take time.

## Our advice

Don't move because a sales rep says you should. Move when the app is causing lost chats, repeat questions or blind spots you can name. Then choose the platform for how your team works, not for its feature list.

Not sure if you have reached that point? Our [customer service audit](/customer-service-audit) looks at your volumes, team and channels. It tells you plainly whether the move will pay for itself.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the WhatsApp Business API free?

No. Meta charges per message for templates, and from 1 October 2026 also for service replies inside the 24-hour window. Providers usually add a monthly fee on top.

### How many people can use the WhatsApp Business app?

One main phone plus up to four linked devices. The API has no fixed limit; it depends on the platform you connect it to.

### Can I keep the same phone number when I move to the API?

Yes. You can connect your existing business number to the API and, through what providers call coexistence, keep using the app on it too. Recent chat history can be synced.

### Can I use a chatbot on the WhatsApp Business app?

Only simple greeting and away messages. A real chatbot needs the API.

### How long does it take to move to the API?

For most small teams, one to three weeks. Verifying your business with Meta is usually the slowest part.
