To reduce call center costs, cut the number of calls before you cut the cost of each call. Remove the reasons people call, stop them calling twice, shorten the calls that remain, and staff for when calls actually arrive. In a typical 20-agent center, that order can save around 30% without making customers wait longer.

Most advice starts with new software, a cloud phone system or outsourcing. Those can help, but only after the basics. Otherwise you pay less per call to answer calls that should never have happened.

## Where the money goes

Almost all of a call center's cost is time: agents on the phone, agents waiting for the phone, and agents in training. Your phone system, helpdesk and scheduling tools come a distant second.

So the question is simple. How many agent hours do you need each month? That depends on three things.

- **How many calls arrive**
- **How long each call takes**, including the work after it
- **How well your rota matches when calls arrive**

Every idea in this guide works on one of those three.

## Five ways to reduce call center costs

### 1. Remove the reasons people call

Listen to 50 calls and write down why each person called. You will usually find that a handful of reasons make up most of the volume. Order status, payment questions and "I didn't get the email" are common.

For each one, ask what would have stopped the call. A clearer confirmation email can remove calls completely. So can a tracking link by text, or a short recorded message about a known delay.

### 2. Stop people calling twice

A caller who rings back about the same problem doubles the cost. Repeat calls usually happen because the first agent couldn't fix it. They lacked the information, the permission, or a quick way to pass it to the right team.

Track repeat calls within seven days. Then give agents the tools and authority to close the most common issues on the first call.

### 3. Make the calls that remain shorter, the right way

Shorter calls save money, but only if the problem still gets solved. Pushing agents to hang up faster just creates repeat calls.

Look instead at what slows calls down: hunting across systems, putting callers on hold to ask a supervisor, typing long notes afterwards. Put customer details on one screen. Write clear answers for the top questions. Use short note templates.

### 4. Staff for when calls actually arrive

Calls come in waves: busy Monday mornings, a spike after a delivery problem, quiet mid-afternoons. A rota built on the daily average leaves callers waiting at 9am and agents idle at 3pm.

Count calls in 30-minute blocks for a month and shape shifts around them. Offer a callback when the queue is long. It keeps callers from hanging up and ringing again, which quietly inflates your volume.

### 5. Move some calls to cheaper channels, carefully

Some questions are better handled by message, where one agent can help several people at once. But don't force it. Customers with urgent or emotional problems still want to talk.

Check the costs too. If you move calls to WhatsApp, replies through the WhatsApp API are charged per message from 1 October 2026. See [WhatsApp pricing from 1 October 2026](/blog/whatsapp-pricing-october-2026).

## Why cost per call is the wrong number to watch

Cost per call rewards you for answering calls cheaply. It doesn't reward you for preventing them. In the example, removing thousands of avoidable calls saves over €10,000 a month, yet cost per call stays the same.

Watch **total support cost per customer** or **per order** instead. That number falls every time a call is prevented, shortened or answered right first time. If you only report cost per call, the best improvements look like they did nothing.

## What we have seen in practice

In one B2B payroll operation we rebuilt, we added a phone menu (IVR), customer satisfaction surveys and self-service for simple requests. Customer service took ownership of requests from start to finish. Incoming requests fell by 45%, and the team got 30% smaller. Response time for priority clients went from nearly three hours to under thirty minutes.

The software came second. The first step was knowing why people called.

This week, pull last month's call volume by 30-minute block and listen to 50 calls. Those two things will show you whether your biggest saving is in fewer calls, shorter calls or a better rota.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the biggest cost in a call center?

Agent time. Salaries and employer costs for the people answering, waiting for and training for calls are usually the largest share by far.

### How do you calculate cost per call?

Divide your total monthly call center cost by the number of calls handled that month. But also track cost per customer or per order, because cost per call doesn't show the savings from preventing calls.

### Does outsourcing reduce call center costs?

It can lower the hourly rate. It won't remove avoidable or repeat calls, and you often lose sight of why customers call. Fix the call reasons first, then compare outsourcing quotes.

### Do callbacks reduce call center costs?

They can. A callback stops people hanging up and ringing again, which inflates volume. It also lets you spread calls into quieter periods.
