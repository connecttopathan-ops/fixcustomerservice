To improve customer service, start by measuring where you are, then remove the reasons customers need to contact you. After that, make every answer consistent, let your team solve problems in one go, and only then work on speed and automation. Most businesses do these in reverse order, which is why their service improves for a few months and then slips back.

This guide gives you eight customer service improvement strategies in the order we use them. Each one says what to do, how to tell if it is working, and what it usually changes.

## Why the order matters

Most advice on how to improve customer service is a list of good ideas. Be friendly, reply faster, add live chat, train your team, use AI. None of them is wrong. But if you do them in the wrong order, each one makes the next harder.

Take speed. Say you hire two people to reply faster, while customers keep writing about the same broken delivery emails. You now pay more people to answer messages that should never have been sent. Fix the cause first and the same team gets faster without trying.

## 1. Measure where you are

You can't improve what you can't see. Before changing anything, write down five numbers for last month:

- How many customers contacted you, on every channel
- Your top five reasons for contact
- How long customers waited for a first reply
- How many contacts were about the same problem as before
- What customers said when asked how it went

If you can't get one of these, that gap is your first finding. Our [customer service audit checklist](/blog/customer-service-audit-checklist) turns this into a score out of 50 in about an hour.

**How you know it's working:** you can answer all five questions in under ten minutes, every month.

## 2. Remove the reasons customers contact you

This is the step most businesses skip, and it gives the biggest improvement. Look at your top five reasons for contact and ask: what would have stopped this customer needing to ask?

- "Where is my order?" usually means the dispatch email has no tracking link.
- "Did my payment go through?" usually means the confirmation page is unclear.
- "How do I return this?" usually means the returns steps are hard to find.

Each fix sits with another team: operations, product or finance. Bring them your top reasons once a month, with the numbers. We explain how to put a price on each one in [how to calculate cost per contact](/blog/how-to-calculate-cost-per-contact).

**How you know it's working:** contacts per order or per customer go down, even as the business grows.

## 3. Make every answer consistent

Customers lose trust fast when two people give them two different answers. Write one clear answer for each of your 20 most common questions, and keep them where everyone can find them. Review them every quarter.

These answers also become your help pages, your quick replies and, later, what a chatbot draws on. One good answer written once saves hundreds of slightly different ones written under pressure.

**How you know it's working:** in a monthly review of 20 random conversations, the same question always gets the same answer.

## 4. Let the first person solve it

The most frustrating thing for a customer is being passed around. Give the person who answers two things: the information to solve the problem and the authority to do it.

Information means seeing the order, payment and past conversations next to the message. Authority means clear limits they can act on without asking. For example: "refunds up to €50 can be approved straight away."

**How you know it's working:** fewer customers contact you a second time about the same issue within seven days.

## 5. Set speed targets you can keep

Now work on speed. Set a target first reply time for each channel, and write it down. For example, one hour on chat and WhatsApp, and four working hours on email. Then look at when messages actually arrive, by hour, and move shifts to match.

Speed alone is not the goal, as we explain in [reply time is not the real problem](/blog/reply-time-is-not-the-real-problem). But a target you keep 90% of the time is a promise customers can rely on.

**How you know it's working:** you meet your reply targets at least 90% of the time, including evenings and weekends.

## 6. Fix complaints at the root

Every complaint is free advice. Give complaints one owner and a deadline. And every month, ask which complaint reasons came up more than once and what will stop them.

For a formal approach, the international standard [ISO 10002](https://www.iso.org/standard/71580.html) sets out guidelines for handling complaints. Its principles work for a team of three as well as three hundred.

**How you know it's working:** the same complaint reason doesn't appear month after month.

## 7. Automate what is left

Automation pays off only after steps 2 to 4. By then you are automating clear, common questions with one right answer, such as order status, opening hours and return steps.

Be careful with customers' trust. In a [Gartner survey of 5,728 customers](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-07-09-gartner-survey-finds-64-percent-of-customers-would-prefer-that-companies-didnt-use-ai-for-customer-service), 64% said they would prefer companies didn't use AI for customer service. Their top worry was that it would get harder to reach a person. So always make it easy to reach a human, and measure your bot on problems solved, not chats started.

**How you know it's working:** the share of chats solved without a person goes up, and satisfaction doesn't go down.

## 8. Review it every week

Improvement fades without a habit. Spend 30 minutes every week on the same five numbers from step 1, plus three conversations read out loud with the team. Pick one thing to change that week, and only one.

**How you know it's working:** the five numbers move in the right direction three months in a row.

In the first rebuild, 1.5 million cardholders moved from email queues to WhatsApp. A chatbot handled common questions and handed the rest to the right agent. The average wait time fell by 96%, as described in [Infobip's published case study](https://www.infobip.com/customer/edenred).

In the second, business clients' requests bounced between support and operations by email, with nothing measured. Nineteen clients left in ten months. After giving support end-to-end ownership, adding tracking and building self-service for simple requests, incoming requests fell by 45% and the team got 30% smaller.

Both started with step 1: finding out what was really coming in, and why.

## Quick wins for this week

If you want to start today, these take less than a day each:

1. Tag the reason for every contact for one week.
2. Add a tracking link to your dispatch email if it doesn't have one.
3. Write a clear answer for your three most common questions and share it with the team.
4. Set one written reply-time target for your busiest channel.
5. Read ten recent conversations with your team and agree on one change.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best way to improve customer service?

Remove the reasons customers need to contact you. Fix the cause of your most common question, such as a missing tracking link. That improves service for every customer and frees your team for harder cases.

### What are some ideas to improve customer service quickly?

Tag the reason for every contact for a week. Write one clear answer for your top three questions. Set a written reply-time target for your busiest channel. Each takes less than a day.

### How do you measure customer service improvement?

Track five numbers monthly: total contacts, top contact reasons, first reply time, repeat contacts within seven days, and customer satisfaction. Improvement means these move the right way for three months in a row.

### Does AI improve customer service?

It can, once the basics are fixed. In a Gartner survey, 64% of customers said they would prefer companies didn't use AI for service. Their main fear was losing access to a person. Automate simple questions and keep a human easy to reach.

### How long does it take to improve customer service?

Quick wins show within weeks. Bigger changes, such as removing common contact reasons and reorganising the team, usually show clear results in three to six months.
